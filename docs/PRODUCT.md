# Wildday product brief

## The idea

People often have goals and tools, but struggle to connect intention to a repeatable system and to resume after disruption. Wildday helps someone decide what matters, make a practical plan, take today's next step, and review what is working.

## Product promise

**Turn what matters to you into a rhythm you can return to.**

The product can support any life area a person chooses—health, learning, work, relationships, creativity, finances, or something entirely personal—without assuming there is one correct definition of productivity.

## Who it serves

People who want more structure and follow-through but need their system to flex with real life. They may be building a routine, preparing for a deadline, protecting focus time, or restarting after a difficult week.

## The core loop

1. **Choose what matters:** capture a goal or desired change.
2. **Make it workable:** turn it into a system with a small, repeatable action and a cadence.
3. **Pick today's move:** see one clear next action alongside the rest of the day.
4. **Do the work:** use a focus session, a simple completion check-in, or a self-chosen reminder.
5. **Learn and adapt:** review progress, adjust the plan, celebrate milestones, and resume without punishment.

This loop is the product's center. Timers, sounds, challenges, notifications, calendar events, and widgets should strengthen it rather than compete for attention.

## First release: prove the loop

### Include

- A short setup that asks what the person wants to improve and when they want to act.
- A Today view with a clear next action and an easy completion or reschedule choice.
- Goals connected to repeatable actions, with flexible schedules and a lightweight progress history.
- A configurable focus timer with work and break intervals.
- Optional local reminders with quiet hours and straightforward controls.
- A weekly reflection that helps adjust the system instead of grading the person.
- Local-first persistence so the core experience remains useful without an account or network.

### Defer until the core loop is useful

- Daily, weekly, monthly, and custom challenges with richer progression.
- Home-screen widgets and quick actions.
- Rain, ambience, and silence or focus modes.
- Birthday and deadline management, calendar connections, and cross-device sync.
- Intelligent recommendations, automations, and integrations with other services.
- Social accountability, shared challenges, and expanded achievements.

These are part of the larger vision, but need to be introduced in an order that preserves clarity and user control.

## Experience principles

- **No punishment loop:** missing a planned action never erases a person's history or demands a streak restart.
- **Smallest useful step:** support a lighter version of a routine for hard days.
- **User-owned accountability:** make reminders opt-in, adjustable, and easy to pause.
- **Progress with context:** show consistency and patterns without pretending a single score can measure a life.
- **Fast setup, deeper customization later:** start useful quickly; reveal advanced options when someone asks for them.
- **Accessible by default:** readable type, clear contrast, screen-reader labels, reduced-motion support, and generous touch targets.

## Questions to answer through prototypes

- Should the main organizing unit be a goal, a routine, or a life area?
- How much should the Today view surface when a person has many priorities?
- Which visual direction makes the product feel motivating without becoming noisy?
- What is the lightest onboarding that still creates a useful first day?
- Which signals should recommendations use, and what must always remain user-controlled?
